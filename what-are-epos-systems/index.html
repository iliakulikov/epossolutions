EPOS guide

What is an EPOS system?

An electronic point of sale system records transactions and connects the everyday tools a business uses to sell, take payment, manage stock and understand performance.

More than a cash register

A modern EPOS system combines software and hardware. The touchscreen or handheld device is the visible part, but the software can also update inventory, send orders to a kitchen, manage customers and produce real-time reports.

Typical EPOS components

  • Touchscreen terminal, tablet or handheld device
  • Receipt printer, cash drawer and barcode scanner where needed
  • Integrated card payment terminal
  • Cloud software for products, staff, stock and reporting
  • Connections to ecommerce, accounting or booking platforms

Who uses EPOS?

Retailers use EPOS for fast checkout and stock control. Restaurants, pubs and cafés use it to manage tables and send orders to the kitchen. Service businesses use it to track sales, customers and staff access.

Cloud or local?

Cloud-connected EPOS lets owners review performance away from the premises and keeps multiple locations aligned. Reliability still matters, so ask how the system behaves if the internet connection drops and what support is available.
